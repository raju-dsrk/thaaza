# Competitors: Pottel vs Licious vs Thaaza

A positioning note for the Hyderabad live-cut meat demo brand **Thaaza**.

## Snapshot

| | **Licious** | **Pottel** (local / regional apps & kirana-style meat delivery) | **Thaaza** |
|---|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|------------|
| Core promise | Premium packaged meat, national brand, cold chain | Convenience meat delivery in city pockets | **Live-cut at neighbourhood butcher shops** |
| Freshness story | Processed / chilled / frozen SKUs with brand QC | Mix of shop partners & packed inventory | Animal seen / cut after order — **not frozen dead stock** |
| Trust cue | App ratings, packaging, certificates | Speed & local familiarity | Visual trust: see animal → choose cut → watch dressing |
| Fulfilment | Delivery-first | Delivery-first | **Visit shop · Takeaway · Delivery** |
| Assortment | Broad (meat, seafood, ready-to-cook) | Often chicken-heavy + mutton | Goat, sheep, broiler, country chicken, eggs, fish, prawns |
| Price posture | Premium | Value / mid | Quality-first mid — not race-to-bottom cheap stock |
| Ops model | Centralised supply + dark stores / hubs | Aggregator / local sellers | Asset-light **partner butcher network** + shared SOPs |
| City focus | Multi-city India | City / micro-market | Start dense in **Hyderabad**, then replicate |

## Licious

**Strengths:** Strong brand recall, polished app, consistent packaging, national playbook, ready-to-cook range.

**Gaps vs Thaaza’s bet:** Many urban Hyderabad buyers still prefer traditional live-cut for mutton/goat. Cold-chain “fresh” can feel opaque if the customer never sees the animal. Delivery-only misses customers who want to stand at the counter.

## Pottel & similar local delivery apps

**Strengths:** Local density, faster last-mile in pockets, often cheaper entry, relationships with existing shops.

**Gaps:** Brand and hygiene storytelling can be thin; assortment and cut quality vary by seller; harder to stand for a single crisp promise (“live-cut, always fresh”).

## Thaaza’s wedge

1. **Promise:** Live-cut. Always fresh. Quality first — not frozen, not cheap dead stock.
2. **Ritual:** See animal → Choose cut → We cut → Pack (codified in UX and SOPs).
3. **Fulfilment flexibility:** Don’t force delivery; visit and takeaway keep the butcher-shop ritual alive.
4. **Hyderabad English + Telugu warmth:** Cultural fit for biryani cuts, natu kodi, paya, rohu/katla.
5. **Investor narrative:** Digitise trust that already exists in neighbourhood shops, rather than replace it with anonymous cold-chain meat.

## Not a claim of superiority in every SKU

Licious wins on national brand systems and RTC. Local aggregators win on pure price/speed in some lanes. Thaaza wins when the customer’s decision is: *“I want this animal cut my way, today, from a shop I can trust.”*
